§ 05 · Beyond the math

Outside the room.

I'm not a nerd who happens to also swim. I'm a swimmer who happens to also do olympiad math, plus a few other things. None of this is on my transcript. All of it adds up to me.

i · swimming

I have voluntarily destroyed my circadian rhythm.

Nine years competitive. About sixteen hours a week in season, which works out to roughly one entire month of waking life per year spent staring at a black line at the bottom of a pool.

WPIAL States qualifier in the 50 and 100 free. I dropped 1.8 seconds at last States to close out a relay with a 22.2 split. The thing I never expected: some of my best math happens in the half-hour after practice — exhausted, walking home, brain too tired to talk itself out of an idea.

States qualifier · varsity 22.2 · 50 free split 16 h/wk · in-season
ii · piano

A long & ongoing relationship with mediocrity.

Ten years. I have the fingers — Chopin ballades, Liszt études, Rachmaninoff preludes, technically in reach. What I don't quite have yet is the musicality. I'm a robot who has read a manual on how to feel things.

I still play almost every day. There's something about working through a piece you can't quite express that keeps a person honest.

Carmel Klavier · GP '19 · 2nd '21 Pittsburgh Int'l · 1st, multiple GP Steinway Society W. PA · audition winner
iii · skiing

The only thing I let myself be reckless about.

Christmas in Colorado — Vail, Breckenridge, Keystone. Spring break at Whistler. Most of my life is a tightly-controlled exercise in not making stupid choices. Skiing is the one place where the answer to "is this a good idea?" is who cares, let's find out.

Purple Haze, Spanky's Ladder, DOA. Not Olympic, not even close. But the version of me on a black diamond in March is somehow the most me version of me there is.

Whistler · spring · back side Vail / Breck / Keystone · winter
iv · card magic

Self-working tricks, hundred-year-old combinatorics.

Tried sleight-of-hand during COVID. Discovered my hands have the dexterity of a polite raccoon. So I switched to self-working tricks — the kind where modular arithmetic and permutation parity do the work and you just deal cards in the right order.

My favorite is the Gilbreath Principle: alternate the deck, let the spectator riffle-shuffle, deal in pairs — every pair is one red and one black. Not marked cards. Just a fact about permutations. There's a working version of it on the Reach page.
