§ 04 · Competitions

The countdown.

Top ten moments, ranked by what they meant. Not what they looked like on paper. Scroll to count down from 10.

The table at the bottom of this page is the resume — every contest, every score, dates, percentiles. Skim it if that's what you came for. The countdown above it is the part I actually care about. Ten moments I'd tell you about over coffee, in the order I'd tell them.

A 5 out of 42 is at #1 instead of a 1st place because that's actually how it shook out. I got a 5 on the USAMO this year. The median was 6. I crashed out for a week. Then, weirdly, I noticed I was happier — the constant am I going to make USAMO in the back of my head had gone quiet, and I hadn't realized how loud it'd been. The 1st place at MathLeague Nationals (that's #3) is the bigger win by any reasonable measure. It didn't change how I think. The 5 did.

A quick map of how long this took. Sixth grade AMC 8: 12 out of 25. I didn't know what \(\binom{n}{k}\) meant. Eighth grade, a teacher pulled me aside and told me to stop dreaming, that AIME wasn't going to happen, that I should focus on academics. Six years between that conversation and the USAMO invite (I'm still a little smug about that one, lmao). The countdown below is most of those six years. Most of it doesn't show up on the cards — late nights in the math room with Ryan Gong, scores I'm still mad about, conversations I keep replaying. Cards are the visible part.

AMC 12B — top 5%

On most years this would have been top 2.5%. This year, with cheating scandals pushing cutoffs through the roof, top 5%. The math wasn't subtle: to qualify for USAMO via the AMC route, I'd have needed a 13 or 14 on the AIME. I'd never broken 8. So I made a decision that sounds insane — I stopped studying for the AIME.

Business case competitions. Twice qualified for the International Career Development Conference — the international round, against teams from every state and several countries. AIME I — school record

Going into junior year my best AIME was an 8. I decided — counterintuitively — to stop studying for the AIME and instead spend my time on the summer-camp applications. Six weeks before the test, I was barely doing mock AIMEs. Walked in cold, walked out with a 12. Biggest single-year jump I've ever made on a math contest. Schoolwide record.

National Champion. 1st in the country. Countdown round semifinalist. Came back twelve months after slipping into Internationals on three hours of sleep, and won the whole thing. Of every competitive result on this page, this is the one I'd hand to past-me — the version of me who, in eighth grade, was told to stop dreaming.

Ross · SUMaC · PROMYS

All three of the big serious-math summer camps for high schoolers — accepted. Picked Ross. Six weeks of pure number theory at Otterbein, this summer. The applications themselves — long essays, deep problem sets — taught me more in six weeks than my entire previous year of AIME prep. They are the reason the 12 happened.

USAMO qualifier — first in school history

Scored a 5 on the USAMO. Median was 6. Walking out I thought my distribution was 7-2-0-7-5-0 — turned out to be 2-0-0-1-2-0. I crashed out for a week. Then I noticed something weird: I was happier. The constant background pressure of am I going to make USAMO had gone quiet, and I hadn't realized how loud it had been. The 5 is at #1 not because it's the best score on this page — it isn't. It's because it taught me the most: that the score isn't the point, and the year still meant something even when the number didn't.

USAMO · 2026·median 6 · first qualifier in school history
